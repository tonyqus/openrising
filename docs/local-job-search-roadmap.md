# Future improvement: local job-search guidance

Status: deferred. These are planning notes, not researched destination recommendations or part of the current job-search chapter.

Recruitment channels differ across countries, cities, occupations and applicant circumstances. A future edition could add a country-level foundation with short city supplements, rather than repeat an entire guide for each city.

## Proposed research structure

| Layer | Questions to investigate |
|---|---|
| Country or region | Which channels serve graduates? What application conventions, languages and recruitment cycles matter? |
| City | Which industries, employers, universities, communities and events are relevant? |
| Occupation | Which channels serve software development, data science or another defined field? |
| Applicant circumstances | How do opportunities differ for local graduates, international students and applicants living abroad? |

Occupation and applicant eligibility may matter more than the city alone. A platform with many vacancies is not necessarily useful to graduates or applicants requiring sponsorship.

## Proposed city card

Each card should answer five questions:

1. Where can the target reader discover suitable graduate vacancies?
2. Where can they verify vacancies and find official application routes?
3. Where can they meet relevant professionals, alumni and recruiters?
4. Which recruitment windows require advance preparation?
5. Which language, residence or work-authorization conditions need checking?

Start with two or three cities where information can be checked with local practitioners. Identify the target reader, record sources and verification dates, and leave unsupported claims unresolved. Evaluate channels by their relevance and accessibility to that reader, rather than platform popularity alone.

Do not publish city rankings or specific local platform recommendations until this research is complete.
