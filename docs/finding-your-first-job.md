# Finding your first job with Indeed, LinkedIn and professional connections

A job search needs both evidence of your ability and a way for employers to discover it. Your [GitHub profile](github-profile.md) and [open-source contributions](building-experience-through-open-source.md) provide that evidence. Recruitment platforms, company career pages and professional connections help you find opportunities and put your work in front of relevant people.

This chapter focuses on recent graduates in software development and data science. Start with a clear target role, read vacancies carefully and apply honestly.

## Give each channel a purpose

| Channel | Main use | A practical first task |
|---|---|---|
| [Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/) | Discover vacancies, including jobs distributed from employer recruitment systems or indexed from career sites | Collect relevant graduate or junior vacancies and identify recurring requirements |
| [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/) | Present your professional background, discover jobs and build connections with people in your field | Complete your profile, link your work and follow target employers |
| Company career pages | Check the employer's own vacancy details and application instructions | Confirm that the position remains open and check eligibility |
| Professional communities and events | Meet people, learn about teams and demonstrate your interests through participation | Attend a relevant event and follow up on a specific conversation |

These channels complement one another. Their usefulness varies by country, occupation and applicant circumstances. This is not a universal ranking of platforms.

## Why Indeed deserves attention: the recruitment systems behind the listings

Indeed may attract less attention in everyday professional conversation than LinkedIn, but its connections to employer recruitment systems are worth understanding.

An **applicant tracking system (ATS)** is software employers use to manage vacancies and applications. With a configured integration, an employer can distribute a vacancy from its ATS to Indeed without manually posting the same vacancy again. Indeed also indexes eligible vacancies from company career sites and ATS feeds.[1][2]

For a graduate, this means Indeed can be an important place to discover newly published opportunities. It does **not** establish that every employer sends jobs there first, or that Indeed always displays a vacancy before LinkedIn or another platform. Publication timing depends on the employer's configuration, distribution choices and processing.

Some names you may encounter:

| Recruitment system | Connection documented by official sources |
|---|---|
| Workday Recruiting | Indeed documents ATS Sync for job distribution and states that its integration is certified by Workday.[3] |
| SAP SuccessFactors Recruiting | Indeed publishes guidance on its SAP recruitment integration.[4] |
| Oracle Fusion Cloud Recruiting | Indeed documents an ATS Sync integration. Treat this product separately from Oracle Taleo; do not assume identical capabilities.[5] |
| iCIMS | Indeed documents synchronization of job postings, applications, candidate information and hiring updates.[6] |
| Greenhouse | Greenhouse documents an Indeed Apply integration.[7] |
| Lever | Lever lists job-board distribution and Indeed Apply among its integration categories.[8] |
| Workable | Indeed describes recruitment integrations with Workable, including application and hiring-workflow features.[3] |

An integration may cover job distribution, application delivery, candidate transfer or hiring-status updates. Those are different functions. An integration label alone does not prove that every function is enabled for a particular employer.

Use Indeed to discover a role, then read the employer's application instructions and check the original vacancy when available. Follow the requested application route rather than assuming you must submit the same application through several channels.

## Read job descriptions before you feel ready

You do not have to wait until graduation, or until you have learned everything, to study vacancies.

Collect a manageable sample of roles you would actually consider. Record the skills that recur, the responsibilities involved and the evidence you could provide. A prospective .NET developer might use this exercise to decide which database, web-development or testing skills to practise next.

Turn those observations into small learning or contribution tasks. Do not attempt to build an enormous project just to cover every keyword.

A job description may describe an employer's ideal candidate. Missing a preferred skill need not automatically rule you out, but explicit eligibility conditions still matter. Never present an unfamiliar tool as one you have used professionally.

## Look beyond the “entry-level” label

Search terms such as **graduate**, **junior**, **entry-level** and **internship** are useful starting points. Read the actual responsibilities and requirements before deciding whether a role fits.

Check:

- Required experience and the level of responsibility.
- Training, supervision and support available.
- Location, working arrangement and language requirements.
- Any student-status or graduation-date conditions.
- Work authorization and any stated sponsorship conditions.

A role being overseas, remote, open to workers in your country and eligible for visa sponsorship are four separate questions. “Remote” alone does not answer the others. Check the vacancy and ask the employer when material details are unclear.

This also matters when considering [geographic arbitrage](geographic-arbitrage.md): a salary advertised in another country is only relevant to your plan if the opportunity is accessible to you.

## Use LinkedIn to make your work understandable

Your LinkedIn profile should help someone quickly understand your direction and inspect the evidence behind your claims.

For a recent graduate, useful material includes education, relevant coursework, internships, small completed projects and specific open-source contributions. Link to your GitHub profile, a merged pull request, a demonstration or a technical explanation where appropriate.

Describe your own part accurately. If you fixed one bug in an established project, explain that fix and its context. Do not imply that you built or maintained the entire project.

Follow employers and people whose work interests you. When contacting someone, explain the relevant connection or question. A short, specific message gives the recipient something concrete to respond to.

## For readers seeking work in China: build real connections

I am not reviewing or ranking domestic recruitment platforms in this chapter.

Based on my experience in the HR industry, my advice to young people seeking work in China is to give more attention to professional connections and relevant offline activities, and to reduce indiscriminate online applications. This is an author's recommendation, not a claim that online applications never succeed.

Start with teachers, alumni, former internship colleagues, technical communities, open-source collaborators, industry meetups and recruitment events. You can begin without an existing professional network: participate, ask thoughtful questions and let people see what you can do.

A practical approach is to:

1. Find a community or event related to your target role.
2. Prepare a brief introduction and one piece of work you can explain.
3. Ask specific questions about teams, skills and recruitment.
4. Follow up respectfully after a useful conversation.
5. When a suitable vacancy appears, ask about the application process or whether a referral would be appropriate, then complete the formal application.

Give people enough information to judge whether an opportunity fits you. Respect their time and their decision about making an introduction. A referral can help your work receive attention; you still need to demonstrate the ability required for the role.

## Keep an application record and learn from it

Targeted applications are easier to improve when you keep a record.

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| Employer and role | Company name, title and vacancy link or reference |
| Timing | Date found, deadline if stated and date applied |
| Eligibility | Location, experience and work-authorization conditions |
| Evidence | Relevant project or contribution and the CV version submitted |
| Channel | Platform, career page, event contact or referral |
| Progress | Response, interview stage, next action and outcome |

Review the pattern after a reasonable batch of applications. Few responses may prompt a review of role selection and CV evidence. Interview feedback may point to a skill or communication gap. These are working hypotheses, not proof of why a particular employer declined you.

Set a sustainable routine: discover suitable roles, check fit, tailor the application using true information, record the result and keep building experience.

## A small first exercise

Choose one target role and collect ten relevant vacancies. Identify three recurring skills, select one achievable contribution that helps demonstrate a skill, and prepare an application supported by that evidence. Also identify one relevant community or event where you could start a professional conversation.

**Make your ability easier to verify, and give the right people a clear reason to consider you.**
